Argentina

Land of tango, beef and endless pampas

Quick facts

  • Capital: Buenos Aires
  • Population: ~46 million
  • Language: Spanish
  • Currency: Argentine peso (ARS)
  • Area: 2,780,400 km²
  • Independence: 9 July 1816

Overview

Argentina stretches along the southern cone of South America, from the subtropical north to the icy tip of Patagonia. It's the eighth-largest country in the world — and one of the most diverse in landscape, culture and climate. Snow-capped Andes in the west, endless grasslands in the center, jungle in the northeast, and glaciers in the far south.

Once a Spanish colony, Argentina gained independence in 1816 and quickly became one of the wealthiest nations in the world, driven by beef, wheat and immigration from Italy and Spain. That European influence still shapes its cities, its food and its accent — but the identity underneath is unmistakably South American.

Today Argentina is a country of contrasts: cosmopolitan Buenos Aires alongside remote Andean villages; world-class football alongside quiet rural traditions; economic turmoil alongside a deep, stubborn love of life. It's a place that gets under your skin.

Culture & traditions

Tango

Tango is a dance — a dialogue without words. Born in the working-class neighborhoods of Buenos Aires in the late 19th century, tango is more than a dance — it's a language of longing, a dialogue without words, a mood, a way of moving through the world. UNESCO recognized it as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2009. Every night, in milongas across the city, locals and travelers still dance it the old way.

Mate

Mate (pronounced MAH-teh) is a shared drink — one gourd, one straw, passed around. It's less about the taste and more about the ritual of slowing down.. It's not just a beverage — it's a social ritual. Offering mate to someone is a small act of friendship, and refusing it is almost rude.

Asado & football

Two things unite Argentines more than anything else: the asado (a slow barbecue of beef, chorizo and offal, cooked over embers for hours) and football. The country has won the World Cup three times — and produced two of the greatest players in history, Diego Maradona and Lionel Messi.

Places to see

  • Buenos Aires

    The vibrant capital — tango halls, café culture, European architecture and a nightlife that starts at midnight.

  • Patagonia

    Glaciers, granite peaks and the legendary Route 40 — the wild, windswept south at the end of the world.

  • Iguazú Falls

    One of the largest waterfall systems on Earth, on the border with Brazil. 275 drops, jungle, and the roar of water.

  • Mendoza

    Argentina's wine capital, tucked against the Andes. Malbec, vineyards, and long, dry, sunny days.

Did you know?

  • Aconcagua, at 6,961 m, is the highest mountain in the Americas — and the highest outside of Asia.
  • Buenos Aires' Avenida 9 de Julio is one of the widest avenues in the world, with up to 16 lanes of traffic.
  • The ballpoint pen was patented in Argentina in 1943 by László Bíró, a Hungarian-Argentine inventor.
  • Argentina has 47 national parks, from subtropical jungle to sub-Antarctic tundra.
  • Patagonia is one of the richest fossil regions on Earth — some of the largest dinosaurs ever found were unearthed here.
  • Argentina consumes more beef per person than almost any other country — over 45 kg per year on average.
