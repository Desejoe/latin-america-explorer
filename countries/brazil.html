Brazil

Home of samba, the Amazon and carnival

Quick facts

  • Capital: Brasília
  • Population: ~215 million
  • Language: Portuguese
  • Currency: Brazilian real (BRL)
  • Area: 8,515,767 km²
  • Independence: 7 September 1822

Overview

Brazil is the largest country in South America and the fifth-largest in the world. It covers almost half of the continent, borders every South American country except Chile and Ecuador, and stretches from the Amazon rainforest in the north to the temperate plains of the south.

Once a Portuguese colony, Brazil gained independence in 1822 and became the only Portuguese-speaking country in the Americas. Today it is the largest economy in Latin America, with major industries in agriculture, mining and technology.

But Brazil is more than numbers. It's a country of music, movement and mix — African, Indigenous, European and Asian influences blended into something entirely its own. Football, samba and carnival are not just traditions here; they are the way the country breathes.

Culture & traditions

Carnival

Carnival is Brazil's biggest celebration — five days of parades, music and dancing before Lent. Rio de Janeiro's samba schools compete in elaborate costumes at the Sambadrome, while smaller cities hold their own street parties (blocos) with millions of people.

Samba

Samba was born in the Afro-Brazilian communities of Rio de Janeiro in the early 20th century. It's both a music genre and a dance — fast, rhythmic, and built for celebration. Today it's recognized worldwide as the sound of Brazil.

Football

Brazil is the most successful national team in World Cup history, with five titles. The country has produced legends like Pelé, Ronaldo, Ronaldinho and Neymar. Football here is not just a sport — it's a national language.

Places to see

  • Rio de Janeiro

    Beaches, mountains and the iconic Christ the Redeemer statue overlooking the city. Rio is Brazil's postcard.

  • Amazon Rainforest

    The largest rainforest on Earth — home to millions of species and one-fifth of the world's fresh water.

  • Iguazú Falls

    Shared with Argentina — Brazil's side offers panoramic views of the 275 waterfalls that make up the system.

  • Salvador

    Brazil's first capital, with colorful colonial streets and the strongest Afro-Brazilian culture in the country.

Did you know?

  • Brazil borders every South American country except Chile and Ecuador — ten countries in total.
  • The Amazon rainforest produces about 20% of the oxygen generated by land plants on Earth.
  • Brazil has won the FIFA World Cup five times — more than any other country.
  • Portuguese is spoken by more people in Brazil than in Portugal — by a factor of about 20.
  • The Christ the Redeemer statue in Rio is struck by lightning several times every year.
  • Brazil has the most species of monkeys of any country in the world.
