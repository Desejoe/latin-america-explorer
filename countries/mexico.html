Mexico

Ancient ruins, spicy cuisine and mariachi

Quick facts

  • Capital: Mexico City
  • Population: ~130 million
  • Language: Spanish (plus 68 Indigenous languages)
  • Currency: Mexican peso (MXN)
  • Area: 1,964,375 km²
  • Independence: 16 September 1810

Overview

Mexico sits in North America, between the United States to the north and Central America to the south. It's a country of extreme variety — deserts in the north, tropical jungles in the south, volcanic highlands in the center, and over 9,000 km of coastline along two oceans.

Long before the Spanish arrived in 1519, Mexico was home to some of the world's great civilizations: the Maya, the Aztec, the Olmec and many others. Their legacy is everywhere — in the food, the language, the architecture and the celebrations.

Today Mexico is the most populous Spanish-speaking country in the world. Its culture mixes Indigenous roots with Spanish colonial influence, producing something vibrant, colorful and instantly recognizable — from tacos to tequila, from mariachi to Día de los Muertos.

Culture & traditions

Día de los Muertos

Day of the Dead is celebrated on 1–2 November, when families gather to remember loved ones who have died. Altars (ofrendas) are decorated with marigolds, candles, food and photos. It's not a sad holiday — it's a joyful reunion.

Mexican cuisine

Mexican food is UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage — the only cuisine in the Americas with that status. Corn, beans, chili and tomatoes form the base, and the variety is enormous: tacos, tamales, mole, pozole, enchiladas.

Mariachi

Mariachi is a style of folk music that originated in western Mexico in the 18th century. A mariachi band is built around trumpets, violins and guitars — and it's the sound most associated with Mexican celebrations.

Places to see

  • Mexico City

    One of the largest cities in the world — built on top of the Aztec capital Tenochtitlán. Museums, food, history.

  • Chichén Itzá

    The most famous Maya pyramid, in the Yucatán Peninsula. One of the New Seven Wonders of the World.

  • Oaxaca

    Mexico's culinary capital — mole, mezcal, and some of the best Indigenous markets in the country.

  • Cancún & Yucatán

    Caribbean beaches, cenotes (natural sinkholes) and coral reefs. A different side of Mexico.

Did you know?

  • Mexico City sinks about 50 cm every year — because it's built on a drained lakebed.
  • Chocolate, corn, chili and vanilla all originated in Mexico and were introduced to the world by the Spanish.
  • Mexico has 35 UNESCO World Heritage Sites — more than any other country in the Americas.
  • The country has 68 recognized Indigenous languages, spoken by over 6 million people.
  • Mexico's national university, UNAM, is one of the oldest in the Americas — founded in 1551.
  • Mexico has the world's smallest volcano — Cuexcomate, near Puebla, at just 13 meters tall.
