Peru

Inca trails, ceviche and the Andes

Quick facts

  • Capital: Lima
  • Population: ~34 million
  • Language: Spanish, Quechua, Aymara
  • Currency: Peruvian sol (PEN)
  • Area: 1,285,216 km²
  • Independence: 28 July 1821

Overview

Peru sits on the western coast of South America, where the Pacific Ocean meets the Andes mountains. The country has three distinct regions — the coastal desert, the Andean highlands, and the Amazon rainforest — each with its own climate, culture and traditions.

Before the Spanish arrived in 1533, Peru was the heart of the Inca Empire — the largest empire in pre-Columbian America, stretching from modern-day Colombia to Chile. The Inca built roads, temples and cities at altitudes above 3,000 meters, and their legacy is still alive across the country.

Today Peru blends its Indigenous heritage with Spanish colonial influence and later waves of migration from Asia and Europe. The result is one of the most distinctive cultures in South America — famous for its food, its textiles and its archaeological wonders.

Culture & traditions

Inca heritage

The Inca Empire ruled western South America for over a century before the Spanish arrived. Their stonework — massive blocks fitted together without mortar — still stands after 500 years and thousands of earthquakes.

Ceviche

Ceviche is Peru's national dish — fresh raw fish cured in lime juice, mixed with onions and chili. It's so important that Peru has a national holiday for it: 28 June, National Ceviche Day.

Textiles

Andean weaving traditions go back thousands of years. In Quechua communities, women still spin and dye wool by hand, using patterns and techniques passed down through generations.

Places to see

  • Machu Picchu

    The 15th-century Inca citadel, high in the Andes. One of the New Seven Wonders of the World and Peru's most famous site.

  • Cusco

    The former Inca capital — colonial architecture built on top of Inca stone walls. The gateway to Machu Picchu.

  • Lima

    Peru's capital and its culinary center. Colonial squares, Pacific cliffs, and some of the best restaurants in the world.

  • Lake Titicaca

    The highest navigable lake in the world, at 3,812 m. Home to floating islands built by the Uros people.

Did you know?

  • Potatoes originated in Peru — the country has over 4,000 native varieties.
  • Machu Picchu was built without mortar — the stones fit so tightly that not even a knife blade fits between them.
  • Peru has three official languages: Spanish, Quechua and Aymara.
  • The Amazon River — the largest in the world by volume — begins in the Peruvian Andes.
  • Peru has 90 of the world's 117 different climate zones — one of the most biodiverse countries on Earth.
  • The highest sand dune in the world, Cerro Blanco, is in Peru — about 2,080 meters tall.
